# The repository's entry for one paper: an example

Bechky, B. A. 2020. Evaluative spillovers from technological change: The effects of "DNA envy" on occupational practices in forensic science. Administrative Science Quarterly, 65(3): 606–643.

What step 10's entry would show for this paper, built by script from the pilot's files: the 28 readings that two models proposed in six runs, each run one chat (the relation test's two planted readings left out), which make ten distinct points. The titles were written for this example; how, and from what, is at the end. The entry's last parts, the author's commentary, the judgments scholars have offered and what became of each alternative, are empty for now.

**What these readings are.** Each is an alternative reading of the paper's own evidence, in the tradition of Black, Carlile and Repenning (2004): an abductive extension or re-theorization of the study, not an error-catching audit or a replication. The author is invited as a co-interpreter of what the field material can further support, and can append a brief commentary to this entry.

## Claims readings engage most often

A reading engages a claim when a coder codes it as inverting the claim, offering another mechanism for it, rereading its evidence or questioning its premise. Two coders, Gemini 4 Argon and GPT 6 Astra, coded every reading; a reading counts here when either coder says so, and the figure for both coders is given beside it. Fable 5.1 proposed 18 of the readings, in 4 chats; GPT 6 Astra 10, in 2.

### Theoretical claims

- **C05** (core). Occupational values filter evaluative comparisons: alignment with valued ways of doing good work supports adaptation, whereas a threat to those values promotes resistance.
  - Engaged by **15** of the 28 readings (8 by both coders): Fable 5.1, 9 of its 18 (chat 23: 2 of 4; chat 26: 2 of 5; chat 27: 4 of 5; chat 46: 1 of 4); GPT 6 Astra, 6 of its 10 (chat 49: 3 of 5; chat 50: 3 of 5).

## How this example was made

- **Titles:** written for this example in a separate session, by Opus 5.5 given only each reading's opening as the placement model wrote it (two sentences: what the reading opens in the argument, and the investigation that would settle it), in at most 12 words and never copying more than five words in a row. Checked by script for length and copying, not for faithfulness to the readings. In the protocol, the model that proposes a reading writes its title (step 6).
- **Reach:** `ranking_check.json`, by `rank_map_readings.py`.
- **Points:** the PI's grouping of the 28 readings into ten points, made in the pilot's matching test (`matching_results.json`, its grouping P, from the filled grouping sheet), each reading's source from `relation_key.json`. In the protocol, a model drafts one standard statement for each point, and two researchers check it (step 9).
- **Reach bars:** one picture per score, in `reach_bars/`, drawn by the script that builds this example: a bar filled to the score, on one gradient that runs the bar's whole length, from slate blue (#94A3B8) through cobalt (#2563EB) and indigo (#1E40AF) to obsidian (#0F172A), so that a fuller bar ends darker.
- **Which claims a reading engages:** the coders' codes of the relation test, as `map_closure_bechky.json` takes them; the claims' kinds and statements from `CLAIM_KINDS_bechky.md`; each reading's chat from `relation_key.json`; each chat's model and prompt from `chats_models_hours.json`.
- **What was coded:** the coders read only part of each reading: for the 19 readings of the plain and places prompts (chats 27, 46, 49, 50), the wager and its evidence; for the 9 cards (chats 23, 26), the wager statement alone. Which claims each reading engages, and the reach, rest on that part; the rest of each reading, which the network shows folded, was never coded.
- **The runs:** chat 23, Fable 5.1, the paper's index files, without the paper (4 readings); chat 26, Fable 5.1, READING_PROMPT.md, with the paper (5 readings); chat 27, Fable 5.1, the plain prompt, with the paper (5 readings); chat 46, Fable 5.1, the places prompt, with the paper (4 readings); chat 49, GPT 6 Astra, the plain prompt, with the paper (5 readings); chat 50, GPT 6 Astra, the plain prompt, with the paper (5 readings). In the protocol every run of a model uses the same registered prompt; these six used four prompts, and one was not given the paper, so their counts are not those of repeated runs.
- **The network:** `CLAIM_NETWORK_bechky.html`, by `build_claim_network.py`, from the frozen index, the settled map and the files above; the same inputs give the same page. It has no script and loads nothing from outside. Each reading in it is shown as the coders read it, then, folded and marked as not coded, the rest of it as its model wrote it, from its source reply.
- Built by script, twice with the same bytes.
