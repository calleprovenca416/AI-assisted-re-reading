### 2. Direct and indirect dependencies are not distinguished

**Location:** Part 1.

Consider the listed questions:

- P014: Does C01 rest on C02?
- P023: Does C01 rest on C14?

C02 concerns DNA profiling becoming an evaluative benchmark. C14 concerns the practices that make DNA evidence appear scientifically objective.

One reasonable interpretation is that C14 helps explain C02, which in turn supports C01.

The instructions do not specify whether a claim should receive a direct edge to another claim when its dependence is mediated through an intermediate claim.

One coder might identify both edges. Another might identify only C01 → C02, treating C14 as an indirect dependency.

This issue could affect many of the 80 pairs.

**Suggested improvement:** Specify whether the desired graph represents direct argumentative dependencies or all dependencies, including indirect ones.

For reproducible mapping, I would recommend **direct dependencies**, with indirect dependencies derived subsequently from the graph.

If indirect dependencies are included, coders should identify the intervening claims.

### 3. The evidential rule is too permissive about shared passages

**Location:** Part 1, evidential test.

The test allows an evidential edge when a claim, or a passage showing that claim, forms part of the support for another claim.

In this paper, many claims are supported by the same empirical episodes and summary passages.

For example, the toxicology episode supports claims about institutional pathways, technique, occupational values, and occupational change.

Consequently, two coders could reason differently about a pair such as P016:

**Does C04 rest on C17?**

C04 describes the importance of technical compatibility. C17 describes toxicologists' responses to an immediate courtroom threat and their technically compatible methods.

One coder could treat C17 as an empirical instance supporting C04.

Another could judge that both claims draw on the same episode but that the paper does not establish a directed dependency between them.

The present rule does not adequately distinguish these interpretations.

**Suggested improvement:** Clarify that sharing evidence is not, by itself, sufficient to establish an edge.

Require coders to identify precisely how the base contributes to the dependent claim. For evidential edges, the base should supply an identifiable case, observation, or finding that the paper uses to establish the dependent proposition.

The task should also explain whether passages labeled `illustrates` in the map sheet can support an evidential edge, and under what circumstances.

### 4. The distinction between mechanistic and evidential dependence is particularly difficult here

**Location:** Part 1, mechanistic and evidential tests.

The task distinguishes between a base claim's outcome being a step in a mechanism and its outcome being an instance of the dependent claim.

This is conceptually sensible, but the paper makes the distinction difficult because institutional pathways, occupational values, and technique operate together.

The map sheet includes reciprocal questions such as:

- P002: C04 resting on C03.
- P049: C03 resting on C04.
- P062: C05 resting on C04.
- P071: C04 resting on C05.

Technique influences responses to institutional pressures, while occupational values are rooted in the techniques practitioners use.

The paper explicitly treats technique and values as intertwined.

This creates several possibilities: mechanistic dependence, evidential dependence, reciprocal dependence, or no directed dependence.

The task does not give enough guidance for distinguishing these in the specific circumstances of qualitative theorizing.

There is an additional technical problem: **C13, C20, and C21 are designated as having `outcome: none`.** Yet they express substantively important propositions about interpretation, the connection between values and technique, and the relative importance of explanations. Under a literal reading, they cannot supply the outcome required by the mechanistic test. MAP_SHEET_bechky

**Suggested improvement:** Define the meaning of `outcome: none` for mechanistic coding.

Either identify the substantive states of affairs asserted by these claims or specify that claims without outcomes cannot receive mechanistic edges.

Also add a rule:

> Do not infer reciprocal dependencies merely because two explanatory factors interact. Evaluate each direction independently and require separate argumentative support.

A worked example involving C04, C05, and C20 would be particularly valuable.

### 5. The strength distinction is ambiguous for comparative cases

**Location:** Part 1, *Strength*.

C21 claims that differences in occupational technique and values account for the observed response pattern more than relative status or power.

The sheet asks whether C21 rests on each of the three occupational cases:

- P036: C21 on C18, narcotics.
- P056: C21 on C17, toxicology.
- P076: C21 on C16, firearms.

Suppose a coder concludes that C21 rests evidentially on all three cases.

How should strength be assigned?

One coder might say each individual case is `contributing`, because the other two remain.

Another might say each case is `necessary`, because each supplies a distinctive part of the three-occupation comparison that no other case supplies.

Both interpretations follow reasonably from the current definition.

The same difficulty arises when distinguishing `comparative` from `evidential` for claims built from cross-case comparisons.

**Suggested improvement:** Define strength in relation to the dependent claim **as a whole**, not simply whether a specific piece of evidence is unique.

For instance:

> `necessary` means that removing the base eliminates an indispensable component of the dependent claim's stated argument. `contributing` means the base adds support or explanatory coverage, but the paper retains an argument for the claim without it.

The instructions should distinguish an indispensable component of a comparison from a unique but replaceable empirical instance.

The `significance` category also needs a tighter rule. A claim should not qualify merely because it makes another claim interesting or relevant; coders should identify an explicit explanatory or theoretical connection made by the author.

## 2. Problems with explanatory factors and the comparative table

### 6. The number and granularity of explanatory factors are unspecified

**Location:** Part 4.

The instructions tell coders to identify as many explanatory factors as the paper uses, without adding their own.

The paper foregrounds three:

**Institutional pathways, technique, and occupational values.**

These are prominent in the abstract and conclusions.

But the empirical discussion also identifies more specific influences, including the immediacy of a challenge, whether it originates locally, compatibility between methods, existing accreditation work, and the ability to preserve occupational autonomy.

Different coders could produce:

- Three broad factors corresponding to the paper's theoretical framework.
- More narrowly specified factors extracted from the detailed case explanations.
- A combination of broad factors and their constituent mechanisms.

These alternatives would generate very different JSON structures despite potentially representing similar interpretations.

Part 4 also allows coders to assign X1, X2, and subsequent IDs freely. Even identical factor sets could therefore produce differently numbered outputs. MAP_TASK

**Suggested improvement:** Separate factor *discovery* from factor *coding*.

If the objective is to assess agreement, provide a predefined factor registry after an initial exploratory coding exercise.

For this paper, a reasonable starting structure would distinguish the three overarching factors and then define any subordinate dimensions.

For example, institutional pathways could encompass the type of institutional route and the immediacy of local pressure.

Coders should know whether these are separate factors or attributes of one factor.

The treatment of pre-existing accreditation work in toxicology should also be specified: is it an independent factor or part of institutional pathways?

### 7. DNA is included as a unit even though it is not a responding occupation

**Location:** Part 5 and the map sheet's applicability instructions.

This is one of the clearest concrete problems.

The map sheet requires coding four units, including `forensic biology (DNA)`.

However, the paper's Table 2 compares **three occupations responding to DNA-centered evaluation**: firearms, toxicology, and controlled substances.

DNA profiling supplies the benchmark. It is not another non-adopting occupation experiencing the same evaluative spillover.

The table explicitly identifies adaptation or resistance for those three occupations, but does not assign a comparable response to the DNA unit. MAP_SHEET_bechky Bechky_spill over

What should a coder enter as DNA's outcome?

One might select `?`, because the paper does not report the relevant response.

Another might select `-`, because no such adaptation occurs.

A third might select `+`, reasoning that DNA methods themselves changed as DNA profiling acquired legitimacy.

The second issue is what to enter for DNA's factor values. Technical compatibility with DNA is not meaningfully comparable when the unit is DNA itself.

**Suggested improvement:** Introduce an `NA` category meaning *not applicable*.

Alternatively, remove the DNA unit from Part 5 and identify it separately as the benchmark unit.

I favor the second option.

Also specify the outcome precisely:

> The outcome is change in local occupational practices in response to evaluative comparisons with DNA profiling during the period analyzed.

Under that definition, the paper supports adaptation in toxicology and resistance in firearms and narcotics.

This also avoids mistaking earlier changes in firearms documentation practices for adaptation to the particular evaluative pressure being investigated.

### 8. Binary factor codes are inadequate for institutional pathways

**Location:** Parts 5 and 6.

The factors are assigned `+`, `-`, or `?`, defined as present/high, absent/low, or not reported.

That works reasonably well for technical compatibility and value alignment.

It works less well for institutional pathways because pathways are fundamentally categorical.

The paper distinguishes scientific criticism, public evaluation, courtroom challenges, and professional-association debates.

For instance, firearms and toxicology both experienced court-related challenges, but the former were largely remote and the latter locally immediate.

Both could receive `+` for courtroom pressure, despite this difference being central to the explanation.

Alternatively, one coder might define the factor as *immediate local courtroom pressure*, producing opposite codes.

Both would be plausible applications of Part 5.

The paper's Table 2 explicitly distinguishes the institutional routes rather than simply scoring their presence. Bechky_spill over

**Suggested improvement:** Allow different factor types.

Use categorical values for institutional route, and separately defined binary or ordinal measures for immediacy, technical compatibility, and value alignment.

Also distinguish evidence that a route was **present somewhere in the occupation's field** from evidence that it directly exposed the MCCL unit to pressure.

This matters because professional associations responded to the NAS report, yet Table 2 does not classify professional-association activity as a direct firearms pathway at MCCL.

The instructions should explicitly establish the geographical and organizational level at which factor presence is judged.

### 9. The contrast-control instructions do not define what counts as "held"

**Location:** Part 6.

CP01 compares firearms and toxicology.

Both were subject to courtroom-related evaluations. But the immediacy and locality of those evaluations differed.

If the factor is "legal evaluation," it could be coded `held`.

If the factor is "institutional pathway," it could be coded `varies`.

If the factor is "immediacy of institutional pressure," it clearly varies.

CP02 creates a similar issue.

Toxicologists had compatible instrumental techniques; narcotics analysts had a compatible instrumental alternative but rarely used it.

Technical compatibility might therefore be `held`, whereas the actual techniques and their position in daily work clearly differ.

The map sheet identifies what makes these episodes comparable, but that does not establish that the explanatory factors themselves are held constant. MAP_SHEET_bechky

**Suggested improvement:** Define `held` and `varies` relative to a fixed operationalization of each factor.

For example:

> `held` means both sides have the same value on the specifically defined factor, not merely that they share a broad institutional context or general characteristic.

Require `unclear` when the paper provides insufficient information about either side.

It would also help to distinguish the contrast pair's background comparability from the explanatory factors being evaluated.

## 3. Construct registry and evidence-handling problems

### 10. The registry's construct-equivalence criterion is underdetermined

**Location:** Part 3.

The registry groups names considered to refer to the same construct.

The instructions allow differences in wording and qualifiers but prohibit grouping things the paper distinguishes or combining a part with its whole.

The boundary is not always clear.

Potentially disputable combinations include:

| Construct names | Source of ambiguity |
|---|---|
| `occupational practices` / `work practices` | Are these interchangeable, or does one refer to a narrower level of analysis? |
| `legitimacy` / `occupational legitimacy` | Is the first a broader concept than the second? |
| `symbolic legitimacy` / `occupational legitimacy` | Is symbolic legitimacy a dimension of occupational legitimacy or simply another label? |
| `professional power` / `power` | Does the unqualified term retain the professional-occupational meaning? |
| `professional authority` / `professional power` | Related concepts, but not necessarily equivalent. |
| `technology` / `technological change` | An entity versus a process. |
| `occupational change` / `task change` | Potential whole–part relationship. |

These terms are all present in the sheet's construct registry. MAP_SHEET_bechky

**Suggested improvement:** Replace the loose equivalence test with an explicit substitutability rule.

For example:

> Merge names only when they refer to the same theoretical construct in the relevant passages and can be substituted without changing the substantive proposition. Do not merge concepts merely because they are closely related, share a causal relationship, or belong to the same conceptual family.

A short set of paper-specific positive and negative examples would be useful.

Also specify a deterministic rule for selecting canonical names. Otherwise, coders might agree completely about a group while selecting different canonical labels.

### 11. The instructions introduce absences and declared gaps without explaining their coding role

**Location:** Sheet-reading instructions and Parts 1–6.

The map task defines declared gaps and absences, and the map sheet includes DG01–DG03 and A01.

But the requested JSON has no explicit fields for them.

It is unclear whether these records should affect edge existence, strength, factor coding, or merely provide contextual qualifications.

One particularly important example concerns firearms examiners from jurisdictions outside MCCL.

The paper reports that these examiners described challenges in court, but the author explicitly states that she lacks data about their local actions.

The map sheet records this as DG03 and A01. MAP_SHEET_bechky

One coder might treat this absence as limiting support for institutional-pathway claims.

Another might disregard it because the task says not to evaluate the paper's evidentiary quality.

Both interpretations are understandable.

There is a related issue with `ventured` claims C19, C22, and C23: the task does not specify whether speculative extensions of the framework may serve as bases for established empirical claims.

**Suggested improvement:** State explicitly how these materials should be used.

I would recommend that gaps and absences constrain what can be claimed about the paper's observed cases, without automatically removing edges based on the author's argument.

Likewise, speculative future applications should not count as empirical support for established claims unless the author explicitly makes that argumentative move.

### 12. Further edges and evidence fields introduce avoidable variation

**Location:** Part 2 and JSON specification.

Coders can add anywhere from zero to ten further edges, choosing those they consider most important.

There is no common candidate set or importance threshold.

Different coders may therefore return non-overlapping but defensible selections.

This is not necessarily poor coding. It is a consequence of the open-ended task.

The evidence fields create a second difficulty.

Each unit has one combined `ids` array, even though its outcome and each factor require potentially different evidence.

The same applies to contrast controls.

Consequently, two coders can provide different ID arrays while supporting the same factor assessments.

**Suggested improvement:** Keep optional edge discovery separate from fixed-pair coding when assessing agreement.

For the comparative table, use evidence fields attached to individual variables rather than one aggregate list.

It would also help to require a page reference for negative dependency decisions, not just positive edges.

Finally, specify that `paper` means the journal's publication year, 2020, rather than the 2019 copyright year appearing on the article's first page.

## 4. The changes I would prioritize

Not all these issues have equal consequences.

**The first priority should be to improve Part 1.** Its four edge types are useful, but the coder needs a clearer distinction between a genuinely directed argumentative dependency, a shared empirical basis, and a broader theoretical association.

I would add several calibration examples drawn from the actual Bechky claims, particularly involving C03, C04, C05, C13, C20, and C21.

These examples should explain why a pair receives an edge or not, why the chosen type wins over alternatives, and why its strength is necessary or contributing.

**The second priority should be Parts 4–6.** The study's explanatory factors should be identified using an explicit hierarchy: overarching explanatory factors, subordinate characteristics, and contextual enabling conditions.

For agreement assessment, the coders should then work with the same factor identifiers and operational definitions.

The DNA benchmark problem should be resolved, and institutional pathways should not be forced into an undefined binary coding scheme.

**The third priority should be the registry.** It needs stricter construct-equivalence rules and a standard procedure for choosing canonical names.

**The fourth priority should be reproducibility of the output.** Optional edges should be evaluated separately, and evidence should be linked to individual coding judgments.

## 5. A practical way to improve inter-coder agreement

I would introduce a short calibration stage before applying the complete task.

Both coders would independently evaluate a small set of difficult claim pairs, one contested construct equivalence, and the two contrast pairs.

The calibration exercise should deliberately include cases involving shared evidence, reverse dependencies, claims without outcomes, and technically compatible occupations that respond differently.

Disagreements would then be used to refine the coding rules—not to impose a particular substantive reading of Bechky's argument.

Importantly, agreement should be assessed separately for:

**Edge existence**, **edge type**, **edge strength**, **construct equivalence**, **factor identification**, and **unit or contrast coding**.

These are different judgment tasks. Combining their discrepancies into one overall agreement measure would obscure the source of divergence.

The free-selection elements, especially `further_edges` and factor discovery, should be assessed using conceptual matching rather than literal JSON identity.

## Conclusion

The mapping task is trying to capture something substantively interesting: the internal architecture of a qualitative argument rather than simply the presence of concepts or evidence.

However, **it currently asks coders to make several interpretive decisions without specifying the level of abstraction at which those decisions should be made.**

The most consequential ambiguity is whether argumentative dependence means that a base claim is indispensable, contributes evidence, supplies an intermediate explanatory step, or merely makes another claim theoretically meaningful.

The next most consequential problem is the failure to distinguish the paper's three overarching explanations from their constituent empirical features before asking coders to classify them.

Finally, requiring binary values for institutional pathways and including DNA as a responding unit introduce avoidable inconsistencies into the comparative coding.

**My principal recommendation is to retain the task's conceptual ambitions but add a much more precise coding manual, with operational definitions and paper-specific calibration examples.** This would preserve the interpretive work that makes the exercise valuable while making disagreements substantially easier to identify, explain, and adjudicate.
