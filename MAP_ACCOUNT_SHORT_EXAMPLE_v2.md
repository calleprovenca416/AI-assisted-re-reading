**How to read an account.**

- **Reach:** what the reading would do to the paper's core claims if it were right, through the ties between claims. *Contested:* the reading challenges the claim itself. *Unsupported:* the claim loses support it cannot do without. *Weakened:* it loses some of its support. *Restated:* a comparison the claim makes would have to be restated. *Idle:* the claim may still hold but loses its point. Two codings of each reading give these labels; where they differ, both are shown.
- **Bears on:** the parts of the paper's argument the reading engages: claims, the ties between them, and the concepts, cases and factors the argument uses.
- **Where the evidence sits:** the paper's passages on which the reading's own evidence rests. The readings cite pages; a model matched those pages and the reading's descriptions to the passages of the paper's index.
- **Outside the paper's argument:** what the reading relies on or proposes that the paper's argument does not contain.
- **Opening:** what the reading opens in the argument, and the next investigation that would settle it.
