## How to read it

- **Read the claims first, once.** They follow below, in full. A paper states its main claims on many pages, so the same few come round again and again.
- **Then the pages.** Under each printed page stand the claims that the extraction locates there, in words, and the fragments it took from that page.
- **The corpus records a page for each record, and never a paragraph.** So the sheet cannot say which paragraph a fragment or a claim comes from.
- **The fragments are numbered in the order in which the extraction recorded them. In this corpus that order goes back to an earlier page once** (at F120). So the order under a page is not a sure guide to the order on the page.
- A stub is the extractor's paraphrase of a passage, not the paper's words. A gloss is the paper's own later reading of that passage, where the extraction kept one; the corpus does not record the gloss's own page.
- A fragment is listed under the first page of its location. Where it runs on, the next page says so.
- The sheet shows what was recorded and where. It cannot show whether a record gives its passage rightly. The check is of coverage: a passage that prints evidence which no fragment gives, or asserts something which no claim gives.

**The scale of the fieldwork,** as the corpus records it from the methods section:

Fieldwork ran for almost two years, with a nine-month intensive stretch. The researcher participated in distributor work and attended both scheduled events and informal upline gatherings, keeping a journal. The interview material came from 17 current distributors and 16 people outside current membership; interviews generally lasted one to two hours, with additional informal conversations. Documentary material included 10 books/booklets, 24 audio tapes, 137 Amagram success narratives, and relevant websites. The paper gives no aggregate number of observation hours.
