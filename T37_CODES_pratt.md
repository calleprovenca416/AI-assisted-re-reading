# Two coders' codes of the readings of Pratt, M. G. 2000. The good, the bad, and the ambivalent: Managing identification among Amway distributors. Administrative Science Quarterly, 45(3): 456–493.

For each reading of the readings file, in its order, two coders' codes, coder A's and then coder B's: the claim the reading bears on most directly (its main claim) and what the reading does to it (its main type), up to two more claims with what it does to each, the page the main type rests on, and the coder's reason. The claims are numbered as in the map. A code is a coder's reading of the reading, not the paper's words.

## The six types

For one reading and one claim, one type:

- `inverts`: the reading holds that the claim's outcome goes the other way
  under the condition the claim names, or that a process the claim states runs
  in the reverse order.
- `other_mechanism`: the reading keeps the claim's outcome but puts a
  different mechanism or cause in place of the paper's.
- `rereads_evidence`: the reading takes passages the paper itself offers for
  the claim and reads them so that they no longer support it as stated.
- `challenges_premise`: the reading questions something the paper's account of
  the claim takes for granted (a premise, a category, a boundary), rather than
  its outcome or its mechanism.
- `complements`: the reading adds a mechanism, a condition or a level to the
  claim, or changes its scope, without denying it.
- `restates`: the reading says what the claim already says.

**If more than one type fits, give the first that fits in the order above.**

## The codes

### R01

- **Coder A:** main claim C07, other_mechanism; also C03, other_mechanism; C34, complements. Page: pp. 462–463. Reason: Rather than treating the four identification types as outcomes of dream building and positive programming succeeding or failing, the reading explains them as sequential stages along a member's tenure trajectory (pp. 461–463, 477).
- **Coder B:** main claim C07, other_mechanism; also C03, other_mechanism; C34, complements. Page: p. 463. Reason: The reading retains the different identification outcomes but explains their distribution through stages of a membership career rather than through the success or failure of dream building and positive programming.

### R02

- **Coder A:** main claim C23, rereads_evidence; also C29, rereads_evidence; C36, rereads_evidence. Page: pp. 475–476. Reason: The reading reinterprets the passages on encapsulated and ambivalent members as two consecutive moments of the same sequence—yielding to an outsider's skepticism in person and adopting upline framing afterward—rather than evidence of two distinct member states (pp. 475–476, 480–481).
- **Coder B:** main claim C29, rereads_evidence; also C23, complements; C06, challenges_premise. Page: p. 480. Reason: The reading treats the workplace denial as an immediate concession that can precede organizational reaffirmation, so the passage no longer demonstrates a distinct ambivalent attachment arising from competing sources of meaning.

### R03

- **Coder A:** main claim C56, inverts; also C27, rereads_evidence; C03, rereads_evidence. Page: p. 477. Reason: The reading reverses the causal sequence on p. 477 by arguing that former distributors left within their first year before repeated socialization practices had run, so early departure prevented identification rather than failed identification causing departure.
- **Coder B:** main claim C56, inverts; also C27, rereads_evidence; C07, rereads_evidence. Page: p. 477. Reason: The reading reverses the proposed sequence by making early departure prevent identification practices from taking effect, rather than making unsuccessful identification produce departure.

### R04

- **Coder A:** main claim C28, rereads_evidence; also C08, challenges_premise; C03, rereads_evidence. Page: p. 479. Reason: The reading re-examines the second disidentified informant's account on p. 479 to show that his opposition was already set during a coercive recruitment pitch, with no printed evidence of a dream, a failed upline relationship, or outside confidants.
- **Coder B:** main claim C08, challenges_premise; also C28, rereads_evidence. Page: p. 463, footnote 7. Reason: The reading questions whether successful recruitment is a uniform category that permits prospecting to be set aside, since joining can express attraction or an attempt to escape coercive pressure.

### R05

- **Coder A:** main claim C02, other_mechanism; also C12, inverts; C24, complements. Page: p. 476. Reason: Keeping the outcome that dream building and positive programming together produce positive identification, the reading replaces the sensebreaking-and-sensegiving meaning mechanism with behavioral commitment generated by freely chosen, costly public actions (pp. 469, 474, 476).
- **Coder B:** main claim C02, other_mechanism; also C11, other_mechanism; C24, restates. Page: p. 476. Reason: The reading preserves positive identification arising through both practices but makes freely chosen, costly enactment and its cognitive justification the mechanism instead of creating and fulfilling a need for meaning.

### R06

- **Coder A:** main claim C29, inverts; also C22, complements; C20, complements. Page: p. 480. Reason: The reading reverses the causal order in the account of ambivalent identification by arguing that not distributing comes first and leaves non-member ties intact because an inactive member never approaches friends and family (pp. 474, 480).
- **Coder B:** main claim C29, inverts; also C06, challenges_premise. Page: p. 481. Reason: The reading makes inactivity preserve outside relationships by preventing recruitment encounters, reversing the account in which retained outside relationships generate conflicting interpretations and intermittent activity.

### R07

- **Coder A:** main claim C09, challenges_premise; also C36, complements. Page: p. 463. Reason: Without disputing that reported finances failed to sort distributors, the reading questions the premise on p. 463 that financial performance is an independent measure separable from identification, since whether a member counts training expenses as costs already expresses their stance toward Amway.
- **Coder B:** main claim C09, challenges_premise. Page: p. 463. Reason: The reading accepts that reported finances failed to sort identification states but questions treating financial results as an independent measure when decisions about counting training costs already express organizational allegiance.

### R08

- **Coder A:** main claim C20, inverts; also C21, inverts; C29, rereads_evidence. Page: pp. 469–476. Reason: The reading inverts the claim that a strong supportive upline bond channels members into organizational activity and encapsulated sensemaking, arguing from pp. 479–480 that the durable sponsor tie with an inactive member holds only because it does not depend on doing the work and brings about none of that conduct.
- **Coder B:** main claim C20, rereads_evidence; also C21, complements. Page: p. 480. Reason: The reading recasts the inactive member's enduring sponsor relationship as support detached from participation, so the strong tie is evidence of what survives inactivity rather than of a relationship channeling the member into encapsulated sensemaking.

### R09

- **Coder A:** main claim C13, rereads_evidence; also C14, rereads_evidence; C01, challenges_premise. Page: pp. 492–493. Reason: The reading re-examines the dream-building outing in the Appendix (pp. 492–493) to argue that it scarcely links wanted possessions to the downline's self and instead builds the mentoring bond and models the upline's manner inside the very first session.
- **Coder B:** main claim C13, rereads_evidence; also C01, challenges_premise; C14, rereads_evidence. Page: p. 493. Reason: The reading reinterprets the shopping session offered as an illustration of dream building as chiefly transmitting the mentor's manner and forming a familial bond, rather than incorporating desired possessions into a deficient self.

### R10

- **Coder A:** main claim C12, other_mechanism; also C27, other_mechanism; C30, complements. Page: p. 464. Reason: The reading keeps seekership among identifying members as the outcome but attributes it to pre-existing discontent with work, finances, or family brought into Amway rather than to sensebreaking unsettling a settled sense of self (pp. 464–467, 470–471).
- **Coder B:** main claim C12, other_mechanism; also C27, other_mechanism; C30, restates. Page: p. 464. Reason: The reading retains seekership but attributes it to dissatisfaction brought from members' prior lives, with dream building supplying language for that dissatisfaction rather than creating it.

### R11

- **Coder A:** main claim C13, restates; also C12, restates; C15, restates. Page: pp. 464–469. Reason: The reading recapitulates the paper's account on pp. 464–469 of how dream building serves as Amway's primary sensebreaking practice by tying desired possessions to the self, creating identity deficits relative to a comfort zone, and renewing those drives with larger dreams.
- **Coder B:** main claim C13, restates; also C15, restates; C17, restates. Page: p. 464. Reason: The reading repeats the account of dream building incorporating aspirations into the self, exposing a shortfall in the current self, and renewing that motivational shortfall through further dreams.

### R12

- **Coder A:** main claim C20, other_mechanism; also C22, other_mechanism; C29, other_mechanism. Page: pp. 472–474. Reason: The reading keeps encapsulation and its alternatives as outcomes but attributes them to how a distributor's pre-existing circle of family, friends, and coworkers reacts when approached rather than to Amway's positive programming (pp. 472–474, 479–481).
- **Coder B:** main claim C07, other_mechanism; also C20, other_mechanism; C28, restates. Page: p. 463. Reason: The reading retains the range of identification outcomes but assigns the decisive causal role to existing outsiders' responses to recruitment rather than to whether the organization's two practices succeed.

### R13

- **Coder A:** main claim C09, inverts; also C28, other_mechanism; C58, complements. Page: p. 463. Reason: Pointing out that p. 463 only observed gross sales volume while leaving net profit and upward transfers unmeasured, the reading holds that whether net money flows to or from a distributor may sort with the direction of their identification.
- **Coder B:** main claim C09, inverts. Page: p. 463. Reason: The reading proposes that financial position could distinguish identification states once measured as net receipts and payments rather than sales volume, reversing the claim that financial performance does not sort those states.

### R14

- **Coder A:** main claim C27, challenges_premise; also C12, challenges_premise; C15, rereads_evidence. Page: p. 477. Reason: The reading questions the premise that sensebreaking is a single unified process, separating stated dissatisfaction with one's present life from excitement about dreams to argue that failed sensebreaking on p. 477 conflates two distinct conditions.
- **Coder B:** main claim C12, challenges_premise; also C13, rereads_evidence; C27, challenges_premise. Page: p. 469. Reason: The reading questions treating excitement about an aspiration and dissatisfaction with one's current life as a single seekership process, arguing that the former can occur without the latter.

### R15

- **Coder A:** main claim C28, rereads_evidence; also C27, rereads_evidence; C57, inverts. Page: pp. 478–479. Reason: The reading re-examines the passages on pp. 478–479 to show that none of the disidentified informants exhibits both surviving dream-inspired seekership and a failed upline bond, arguing that the passages instead separate opposed from detached leavers by whether they experienced a wrong at the hands of recruiters or upline.
- **Coder B:** main claim C28, other_mechanism; also C27, other_mechanism; C58, complements. Page: p. 479. Reason: The reading explains oppositional departure through a perceived wrong inflicted by recruiters or uplines, regardless of continuing desire for personal change, instead of successful sensebreaking followed by failed sensegiving and outside interpretation.

### R16

- **Coder A:** main claim C07, challenges_premise; also C01, challenges_premise; C57, inverts. Page: pp. 462–463. Reason: The reading challenges the premise that dream building and positive programming are two separate practices, arguing that dream building occurs inside the mentoring relationship so that the two-by-two model collapses into a single dimension of upline bond quality (pp. 462–466, 477–482).
- **Coder B:** main claim C01, other_mechanism; also C07, other_mechanism; C13, challenges_premise. Page: p. 481. Reason: The reading replaces the paired causal processes with the quality of the upline relationship as the single mechanism producing dreams and organizing identification outcomes.

### R17

- **Coder A:** main claim C09, rereads_evidence; also C07, other_mechanism; C28, other_mechanism. Page: p. 463. Reason: The reading reinterprets the evidence on p. 463—that distributors could not or would not state net profits, disagreed over counting training materials as expenses, and earned minimal gross income—as showing that identification types reflect whether members have reckoned their financial losses rather than that finances are irrelevant.
- **Coder B:** main claim C09, inverts; also C11, other_mechanism; C28, other_mechanism. Page: p. 463. Reason: The reading makes the recognition and interpretation of financial costs distinguish positive, oppositional, and inactive attachments, reversing the paper's exclusion of finances as a determinant of identification states.
