# Readings of Bechky, B. A. 2020. Evaluative spillovers from technological change: The effects of "DNA envy" on occupational practices in forensic science. Administrative Science Quarterly, 65(3): 606–643.

8 readings of this paper, in no particular order. Each is split
into numbered sentences. A page in a sentence is a pointer, not a proof.

## Reading W01

W01-01  The narcotics unit kept crystal tests because, on a legal clock and with two instruments, it could not clear its cases any other way; that is the reason an analyst gave when describing a hearing that contested the method.

W01-02  On this reading the unit was neither sheltered inside a professional debate nor chiefly defending a craft.

W01-03  It was holding a position set by statute and equipment while pricing the cheapest way to leave a reviewable record.

W01-04  Its veteran analyst expected the move to instruments, and given the capacity most of the unit would make it.

W01-05  The law requires quick turnaround because charges depend on which drug and how much; the commonest drug is confirmed by crystals in 10–15 minutes, and GC/MS is already used for samples crystals cannot settle (p. 629).

W01-06  This sets the clock and shows the instrument in use, without recorded objection, where it is needed.

W01-07  A veteran analyst names the method's weakness (nothing others can check later), expects the unit will probably have to move to GC/MS, and objects that it takes longer (p. 631).

W01-08  Compliance is anticipated, with time as the stated cost.

W01-09  Describing a workshop on a Daubert hearing against crystal testing, an analyst says the county could not keep up without crystals, having two instruments and long runs (p. 632).

W01-10  Capacity is the reason given, in a court context.

W01-11  A second viewer would double analysis time, and microscope cameras, though costly, would be far cheaper than more instruments (p. 632).

W01-12  Analysts are weighing fixes by time and money and already considering change.

W01-13  Analysts time themselves and press for fixes to a new records system that slowed their cases (p. 631).

W01-14  Speed is a standing concern, one the paper lists among the unit's values (p. 630), though Table 2 (p. 633) names only autonomy and variety as what the comparison offends.

W01-15  Table 2 records no court pathway for narcotics, and p. 635 says the unit met no court challenges of any kind; challenges to firearms evidence, likewise in other jurisdictions and relayed at a professional meeting (p. 621), are coded yes (p. 633).

W01-16  This is the coding the passage unsettles.

## Reading W07

W07-01  The narcotics unit did not turn the benchmark down; it could not afford the one way of meeting it that the paper counts as change.

W07-02  The law in its jurisdiction sets a short clock on drug cases, the unit has two instruments, and a crystal test takes minutes where an instrument run takes far longer.

W07-03  A veteran analyst herself states what the crystal test lacks, an output that someone else can check later, and the unit weighs cheaper ways of supplying one, such as cameras on the microscopes.
