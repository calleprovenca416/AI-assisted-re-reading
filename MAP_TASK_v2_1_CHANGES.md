## What changed

| where | v2 | v2.1 | why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Part 3, the registry | Two names one claim lists side by side are two; a qualifier that marks a kind stays. | Two names are two only where a claim's statement names them as two: one acting on the other, one set against the other, or two separate items. A claim's list of constructs is not enough on its own. A qualifier marks a kind only where the paper sets the qualified name against another kind of the same thing, or says the general term covers more; a qualifier some claims add and others drop names the same construct. | The PI's grouping (S021, S023). v2 kept symbolic legitimacy apart because the sheet's construct list for C09 holds both names, though C09's statement names one thing, an occupation's symbolic legitimacy. |
| Part 1, setting Ci aside | Ci's support is set aside; a passage listed under both Ci and Cj stays with Cj. | Ci's statement goes with its support. A passage listed under both stays with Cj as Cj's own; one listed under Ci and a third claim stays with that claim. Where Cj takes Ci's outcome as a step, Cj loses that step even when every passage under Ci is also under Cj. | The check's ambiguities 1 and 2: Pratt S024 was left open, and S020 and the C01 items turned on it. |
| Part 1, strength | A passage listed under Ci is not another source. | A passage listed under Ci and not under Cj is not another source; a passage listed under both is one when it gives Cj, by itself, what Ci gives. | Ambiguity 1: the two sentences gave different strengths for Pratt S024. |
| Part 1, direct ties; the example | No rule for a pair where the paper draws on both Ci and Ck. | The same test decides it: with Ck kept and Ci set aside, the tie runs through Ck if Ck still gives Cj everything Ci gave, however often the paper names Ci; if Ci gives something Ck leaves out, Cj rests on Ci directly, with a strength for that alone. The example's C5 on C9 shows it. | Ambiguity 3: the check's verdicts for coder A on Pratt S004, S005, S007, S008, S009, S019 and S022 relied on a reading the text did not state. |
| Part 2 | Ten edges, or all if fewer; an edge left out of the ten is not a no. | Up to ten, or those found if fewer; an edge not given is not a no either way. | Ambiguity 4: Bechky S016, S019 and S020, where the coders gave 6 and 5. |

Two lines were rewrapped after these changes, with no change of words.

## What it costs, and what it does not do

- **Length:** 15,547 bytes and 2,815 words, against 13,987 and 2,517 in v2 (10,194 and 1,768 in `MAP_TASK.md`).
- **Fitted to the PI's reading.** The part 3 rule was revised to give the PI's answer on two items. The check can show only whether the text now decides the 65 items as the PI settled and revised them, not whether the rule holds on another paper; a qualifier's kind now turns on what the paper sets against it, which a coder must still read.
- **The check** of the revised text, given only `MAP_TASK_v2_1.md`, the two map sheets and the two settle keys, is in `MAP_TASK_v2_1_CHECK.md`.
