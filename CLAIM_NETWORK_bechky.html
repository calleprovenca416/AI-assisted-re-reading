The claims and the readings that engage them

Bechky, B. A. 2020. Evaluative spillovers from technological change: The effects of "DNA envy" on occupational practices in forensic science. Administrative Science Quarterly, 65(3): 606–643.

What these readings are. Each is an alternative reading of the paper's own evidence, in the tradition of Black, Carlile and Repenning (2004): an abductive extension or re-theorization of the study, not an error-catching audit or a replication. A reading engages a claim when a coder codes it as inverting the claim, offering another mechanism for it, rereading its evidence or questioning its premise. The paper's author is invited as a co-interpreter of what the field material can further support, and can append a brief commentary to the paper's entry.

Each circle is one of the 23 claims of the paper's index. Its size shows how many of the 28 alternative readings engage it, as either of two coders (Gemini 4 Argon and GPT 6 Astra) codes them; the number inside is that count. Colour gives the claim's kind, and a dark ring marks the paper's core claims. A line joins two claims when the settled map says the upper one rests on the lower one: solid when the support is necessary, dashed when it contributes. The lines are the map's ties, not shared constructs; each claim's constructs are listed in its panel. Hover a claim for its statement; click it for its panel, and a reading's title for the reading in full.

What was coded. The two coders, whose codes give the claims each reading engages, the labels and the reach, read only part of each reading: for the 19 readings of the plain and places prompts, the wager and its evidence; for the 9 cards, the wager statement alone. Each reading's panel shows that part first, word for word; the rest of the reading, shown folded, was never coded.

The paper's words. Where a reading repeats six or more of the paper's words in a row, they are highlighted like this, with the paper's page: 21 runs in 10 readings, one of them standing twice in its reading, found by checking every string on this page against the paper. Before any public version, each is replaced by a note giving its length and page.
