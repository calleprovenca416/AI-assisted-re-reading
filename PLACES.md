# Places

4 places in the paper. Each gives a claim the paper makes, as a reader summed it up, with what it says is affected and which way; a passage of the paper, by its page and a reader's summary that points to it; and two readings of what the passage shows. Both readings have fair ground in the paper. Where a summary and the paper differ, go by the paper.

## Place 1

**Claim** (p. 606; pp. 607–608; p. 634; p. 638): Evaluative spillovers allow a technology to reshape the work of adjacent occupations even when those groups do not adopt the technology.

**What it says is affected:** work practices of neighboring non-adopting occupations. **Which way:** when a technology becomes an evaluative benchmark in a shared field, pressure on the work practices of neighboring non-users increases.

**The passage:** p. 627, the one this summary points to: "Before the NAS report, MCCL was already adapting to ISO 17025 and toxicology had a multi-year plan to estimate measurement uncertainty; when the local court challenge became imminent, the unit accelerated that plan and prepared the needed statistical analysis."

**Reading A:** The passage shows work practices of neighboring non-adopting occupations going the way the claim says.

**Reading B:** The passage limits the claim: it shows where or when the claim does not hold, or holds only in part.

## Place 3

**Claim** (p. 625; pp. 627–628; pp. 633–637): At MCCL, toxicologists accelerated a reporting change because a local court threat was immediate, the change fit their instrumented method and error-avoidance values, and related accreditation work was already underway.

**What it says is affected:** local toxicology practice change. **Which way:** an immediate local challenge combined with technical and value compatibility and prior preparation leads to more rapid local toxicology practice change.

**The passage:** pp. 635–636, the one this summary points to: "the author's account is that direct courtroom exposure created a change opportunity for toxicologists, whereas MCCL firearms examiners lacked that local exposure and did not treat colleagues’ distant challenges as a pressing local problem."

**Reading A:** The passage shows local toxicology practice change going the way the claim says.

**Reading B:** The passage is about what the claim is about, but shows nothing of local toxicology practice change itself.
