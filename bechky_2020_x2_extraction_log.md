**Direction decisions and close calls (reasoning before codes):**

- **C01 / C26:** The firearms and narcotics accounts explicitly report retaining local work procedures despite external comparison (F77, F117, F140, F142). That is contrary to the broad expectation that the comparison yields alteration (**`against`** C01), but consistent with the more specific observation that distant, non-urgent challenges rarely occasioned local practice change (**`with`** C26). This is not an assertion that the overarching spillover mechanism is universally false.
- **C03, C27, C28, C29:** The outcomes are *willingness*, *numeric reporting*, *implementation speed*, and *approval*, respectively; these are not interchangeable with the mere fact that a unit introduced some new practice. Thus a report of completed calculations before testimony (F88) establishes accelerated work but is only an illustration of completed numerical reporting; the new uncertainty figure actually displayed (F92) supplies that outcome. Likewise the local toxicity response in Table 2 (F141) does not itself establish approval or precise speed. The note about the county being ahead of others (F139) is not proof of implementation speed or individual approval.
- **C04:** Crystals could be replaced or supplemented in several ways but technicians described duplicate review as slower (F113) and sole reliance on limited GC/MS machines as infeasible at the current workload (F112). These stubs directly report difficulty in altering technique (**`with`**); expressions of disliking repetitive tasks and unchanged practices lack a direct estimate of adaptation *ease* and are **`illustrates`**. This keeps technological practicability distinct from workers' consent.
- **C17, C22, C31, C39:** A public benchmark or a debate about more reviewable evidence does not by itself show an increase in perceived *objectivity*, an individual examiner’s worry, legitimacy of a *specific* instrument, or a *rising number* of affected fields. The corresponding borderline links were kept as **`illustrates`** when the specified outcome was not present in the stub.
- **C35:** Online professional deliberation over second-person crystal review (F113) expressly gives an alternative, non-local pathway for discussion, so it is **`against`** the claim that locally immediate evaluation supplies the discussion opportunities. The separate camera discussion (F114) provides no independently stated causal pathway in its own stub; because it nevertheless reports the outcome, its link is **`with`**, following the brief’s outcome-without-cause rule. Neither decision draws on a second fragment to fill a missing condition.
- **C02:** Table 2 expressly says the academy report did not target toxicology or narcotics, and that some channels of pressure did not affect these groups (F122, F123, F125, F126, F129). These are coded **`qualifies`**, rather than treating every part of the non-DNA field as uniformly criticized through every institution.
- **Scope of `illustrates`:** Routine laboratory procedures and the sample visuals often demonstrate the conditions or objects being compared, but provide no observation of the linked claim's affected outcome. They were not automatically scored `with` simply because the general interpretation fits the author's account.
